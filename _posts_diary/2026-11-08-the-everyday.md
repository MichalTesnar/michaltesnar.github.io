In one of my first weeks after arriving in Groningen in the Netherlands, I went to one of the opening events of the year and bumped into a motivational lecture. I vividly remember the speaker asking us: "How do you imagine your October Wednesday five years from now?" The point of the exercise was to inspect what makes us happy when we remove all the shiny things: positions, awards, goals and so on. What is left then?

Well, it has been five years since then, and October just passed, so I thought it would be a good moment to quickly revisit the idea and reflect on where those five years have taken me.

1. _I usually have low confidence about what is gonna happen in the future._ I am ready to try my best at all times and I am definitely ambitious, but I like to expect nothing. I think that is awesome, since I keep exceeding my expectations and am therefore happy about my progress. Five years ago, I remember the speaker asking us what salary we would like to have. I said 1,000 EUR per month. As you can see, I had no idea what salaries were like, but I guess I wasn't set on making a lot of cash anyway.

2. _I am not sure I want to know what the future is going to bring._ It turns out things come when they have to come, whether you want them or not. I honestly go crazy when I think about how far AI has moved over the last five years. We now talk to computers like it's the most normal thing; if you had told me that five years ago, I would not have believed you. Since we cannot know the future, we should let life unfold, sense where it is taking us, and then always take one step in that [direction](https://www.henrikkarlsson.xyz/p/unfolding).

3. _Planning activities, not outcomes._ Goals help us set a focus and measure progress; however, they can underdeliver on meaningfulness, as the joy comes from doing things, not achieving them. Recently, I was listening to Andrew Ng talking about the [Saturday Morning Problem](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=733m6qBH-jI): your gains compound a lot over time, so you should get into the habit of spending a couple of hours every Saturday morning on what you want to get better at (in his case, research). This also fits well with Kenneth Stanley's mindset: you should not plan for goals, plan for the process, and let the great things happen!

So how do my Wednesdays look five years later? Completely different than I had imagined, but every day is so rich that I cannot do anything but be thankful for it! See you in 5 years :)
