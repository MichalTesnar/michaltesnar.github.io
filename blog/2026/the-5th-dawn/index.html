The 5th Dawn

Created on November 15, 2026

2026

Recently, I have been extremely excited about the future, so I could not wait to find some time to write up this brain dump. Even as I am writing this, I am having problems calming my mind cause I think this is so exciting, and so many new ideas are surfacing right now!

When I was joining the Bachelor’s of Artificial Intelligence, I used to say: “I study AI, cause it will be the next big thing, and I want to be part of the revolution.” I did not have a particular plan. Looking back, I should have bet on this belief and bought NVIDIA, but I do not think I really knew what GPUs were good for anyways back then, all I had was a hunch. This has turned out to be (for better or worse) a good one. Maybe it was not in the form which we expected, as back then people were talking about displacing drivers and image recognition in radiology, but it is true nonetheless.

For about two years now, I have been active in the industry and I had the privilege to work on quite cutting-edge stuff. This is a fraction of the time compared to people before me, but I want to recap what I have learned in these years, and what I think is coming next, and why that makes me incredibly excited.

4 + 1 Revolutions

Many would agree that the publication of ChatGPT has been a turning point for both industry and research of Artificial Intelligence, the tilt towards natural language processing in shared science has been big, and this has pushed capabilities far beyond what we expected (e.g. the METR metric). Here, I would like to track a couple of important points that have shaped the revolution as I have observed them.

Reasoning

When OpenAI released its first reasoning models, there was a rise in STEM capabilities, which I started using for my math homework. However, it really hit me when I started an internship at Oracle on implementing reasoning in their agentic framework. Back then LLMs could do small things here and there, but not whole chains of actions together, which is what came next.

Tool Calls

First just a couple of them, chained together. As part of my curriculum, I was doing a project at the ETH AI Center, where we were generating tool-calling data for the Swiss LLM. I did not realize it, but efforts like this led to LLMs being able to execute actions in code environments, results of which were to drop soon after. I had a short part-time job at Lightly, where we were building reinforcement learning environments for LLMs. From those, LLMs have learned to execute more and more complex tasks.

Reasoning Generalization

Then I joined Tufa Labs, which, apart from using Claude Code every day, gave me the possibility to try to work at the limits of reasoning and tool-calling at the same time. We were working on creating agents for ARC-AGI-3, which to my surprise is best solvable with the language priors. The real progress on this task, sadly, came with better pre-trained compressed base models with accelerated inference. Clear example of the bitter lesson of AI, and stunning progress right in front of my eyes.

Research and Recursive Self-Improvement

All of this has given a massive boost to computer science research: you can now prototype with lightning speed. Drop a paper into a chat window, and it will rebuild the computational experiment, re-run it and discuss the results with you. Seeing how people use it to do science has been crazy. The explosion of submissions to conferences just proves the point. The next goal seems to be recursive self-improvement (RSI), just AI researchers genuinely trying to automate themselves out of their own jobs.

5th One

I am trying to argue there is another one coming our way, a fifth one that I am observing right now, and that is deep understanding of multimodality and physical reasoning. This started with applications of ChatGPT 6 Astra to physical tasks which have yielded stunning results in manipulation tasks. You might argue that it is too slow and too expensive, but papers like ZAP show that you can do it fast and cheap. The newest release of Claude Fable 5.2 is adding numbers on robotics tasks, which means that the companies are aware of this and are even collecting data / training on this. The only edge that open-source might have here is the inference speed, as running real-life applications over the network might be too slow. Regardless of that, the models are slowly taking a crack at physical intelligence, and I think this is a big deal. As reasoning tasks in digital environments are starting to be solved, this has been something where humans could find themselves being useful (my first part-time job was cleaning windows). If that can be solved it will be huge!

Extrapolation

I hope you are still holding onto your papers, cause I will make the wild guess of predicting the future. Even if we stopped the improvement of models now, what kind of impact can the models make? What I am trying to get at is: how long till I can run something like Astra on hardware that a humanoid can carry around? I care about this cause it is just how much intelligence you could compress into a moving thing, that can sustain itself for hours, not just a fixed datacentre. You can also think about packing this level of intelligence behind a checkout desk, or in any other business for that matter. For these purposes, Claude told me the biggest backpack you can fit on a Unitree humanoid is Jetson Thor with 128 GB VRAM. How long till I will be able to fit ChatGPT 6 Astra onto this?

Let’s extrapolate Epoch AI’s index data which is a general intelligence index using averaging of many benchmarks. Now according to this index, there seems to be a clear trend, and we should get such capability around mid-2028. I would say it is actually a conservative guess given some prophesied recursive self-improvement. I take it more as a guarantee that this is more likely to happen, since technical labour and research are getting cheaper and cheaper.

You might just as well say now, “Token speed is all you need.” and optimize hardware for token speed and you get massive real-time intelligence, with little sacrifice on the capabilities. The possibilities waiting ahead of us are great!

P(doom) = 0

You might be now like “f***, not even being a plumber is safe”. But I think we actually have more reasons to be excited than to be sad, the world is going to be an awesome place to live in. Let me give you my reasoning why:

  1. We are not in survival mode anymore. We are taking off 2 days a week. We care about our mental health. We go on vacations. I falsely associated a job with the sense of adding value to the world, but I am more and more convinced that they are here for us to search for information and keep our primate brains entertained. Some would argue that most jobs are already bullshit, I think they are just shaped such that we can distribute money among ourselves. Crises like Covid told us how fast things can go if we really want to change something, the world is moving really slow without a real sense of urgency. Therefore jobs being taken away just means we have to find new ways of attributing value to individuals.

  2. Access to commodities will be greater than ever before. Buying enough calories to feed you for a day is a triviality in most western countries (not saying it is good calories though), with the internet, you can access basically all knowledge of the world for a couple cents. What else can we unlock when labour is basically free? When we remove the legal barriers to autonomous driving, and start trusting it more? We will be able to get so much done! The unlocks are going to be great, we will basically live in a world all our ancestors dreamed of (maybe dreaming was important after all?). One particular consequence of this that excites me is the fact that it has never been easier to learn something, you literally have the most knowledgeable teacher online at your disposal all the time – the bottleneck is just your own motivation (more on that coming in the near future).

  3. You can literally have an army of minds at your command. Like think of all the things that you could do if you had infinite cognitive power. Recently, a colleague of mine used agents to find an unmapped Maya site from lidar data. In the end, the site turned out to be already mapped, but the point is that you can literally have hundreds of agents checking images for you, which 50 years ago you would need humans for. There are a lot of science bottlenecks out there in the wild due to just researchers having old techniques of collecting and analyzing data. Imagine now you just spawn a fleet of agents, soon maybe robots, and they just do it for you. Sequoia’s AI Ascent talk shows this graph of human labour during the industrial revolution, and how once the steam engine took over, the human part diminished. Same goes now for cognitive labour!

THIS IS INCREDIBLY EXCITING! As programmers, we have had the privilege of giving up our entitlement to a job first. This leaves us only wondering what the future will hold. The diffusion and adoption of the technology is going to take time, but that does seem to be the only barrier. We should be building companies & organizations that scale with this observation. In the short term, human labour will most likely move to higher-level product design skills rather than typing code or just basically collect data for whatever the next bottleneck is. However, the economic incentive of AI companies will be to automate anything that has value and for which data exists, so that will not hold for long.

Conclusions

This does not mean living will be easy: if anything, there will be disruption, maybe even conflicts over a new world order. Humans will not be the smartest minds on the planet and this will have consequences. I already hear you saying “yeah yeah, you are just AI-pilled”. The AI companies, the venture funds, the startups, they have an interest in making you believe this. And you are completely right. But look how far we have come, and the progression we made from stochastic parrots to autonomous actors. We might run out of capital, places to build datacenters, or public acceptance of spending resources. Even more so, the investment bubble may pop, but LLMs are here to stay, and they are going to get more efficient.

As Antonio Machado says: “Caminante, no hay camino, se hace camino al andar.” It is indeed nice to look back at how far we have come with AI, and generally as civilization. Pretty great run so far!

As we wait for the AI revolution, I highly recommend listening to some AI Doompop, AI-generated songs about how AI is going to wipe out humanity. The I’m upping my P(doom) is my personal favourite.
